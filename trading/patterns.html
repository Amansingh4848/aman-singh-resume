Reversal01

Double top

Two nearby highs with an intervening low after an advance. The low is often called the neckline.

Study the context & failure +

What to investigate

Define the two-high tolerance, intervening swing and a break rule before testing.

How it can fail

A second high alone is incomplete. Price can break above both highs or reclaim the neckline.

Practice: find one example that fits and one that fails. Fix your rules before revealing the later candles. Illustrations show geometry, not recommended entries or price targets.

Reversal02

Double bottom

Two nearby lows separated by a rebound after a decline.

Study the context & failure +

What to investigate

Locate the intervening high and compare the lows within a zone, not an exact tick.

How it can fail

A second low can be followed by a fresh low; an apparent neckline break can reverse.

Practice: find one example that fits and one that fails. Fix your rules before revealing the later candles. Illustrations show geometry, not recommended entries or price targets.

How it can fail

Repeated tests can end in an upside breakout instead of reversal.

Reversal05

Head & shoulders

Three peaks: a higher middle peak between two lower shoulders.

Study the context & failure +

What to investigate

Mark a neckline connecting the pullback lows; it can slope. Study closes and failed breaks.

How it can fail

The right shoulder is uncertain until later price develops; an upside recovery invalidates a bearish reading.

Practice: find one example that fits and one that fails. Fix your rules before revealing the later candles. Illustrations show geometry, not recommended entries or price targets.

Reversal06

Inverse head & shoulders

Study the context & failure +

What to investigate

Mark the rebound highs forming the neckline and define completion in advance.

How it can fail

A breakout can fail, or price can return to the head region.

Practice: find one example that fits and one that fails. Fix your rules before revealing the later candles. Illustrations show geometry, not recommended entries or price targets.

Reversal07

Rounding bottom

A gradual transition from falling prices through a base into rising prices.

Study the context & failure +

What to investigate

Use enough surrounding history to distinguish a base from a short bounce.

How it can fail

The curve can flatten or resume the downtrend; smooth textbook arcs are rare.

Practice: find one example that fits and one that fails. Fix your rules before revealing the later candles. Illustrations show geometry, not recommended entries or price targets.

What to investigate

How it can fail

A pause can resolve upward and negate the rounded-top interpretation.

Continuation / two-way09

Ascending triangle

Rising swing lows meet a roughly horizontal upper boundary.

Study the context & failure +

What to investigate

Watch both boundaries. A break can occur in either direction despite the common bullish interpretation.

How it can fail

Price can fall through the rising lower boundary or briefly break resistance and return.

Practice: find one example that fits and one that fails. Fix your rules before revealing the later candles. Illustrations show geometry, not recommended entries or price targets.

Continuation / two-way10

Descending triangle

Study the context & failure +

What to investigate

Define a boundary-break rule and distinguish an incomplete contraction from a completed break.

How it can fail

Price can break upward or reclaim the lower boundary after a false breakdown.

Practice: find one example that fits and one that fails. Fix your rules before revealing the later candles. Illustrations show geometry, not recommended entries or price targets.

What to investigate

The contraction describes compression; it does not determine the eventual direction.

How it can fail

Breakouts can fail and late apex noise can be hard to interpret.

Practice: find one example that fits and one that fails. Fix your rules before revealing the later candles. Illustrations show geometry, not recommended entries or price targets.

Reversal12

Rising wedge

Both boundaries rise while the swings contract. Often studied for weakening upward progress.

Study the context & failure +

What to investigate

Draw both rising boundaries with multiple touches and assess the preceding trend.

How it can fail

It can continue upward. A shape alone is not a confirmed downside reversal.

Practice: find one example that fits and one that fails. Fix your rules before revealing the later candles. Illustrations show geometry, not recommended entries or price targets.

How it can fail
