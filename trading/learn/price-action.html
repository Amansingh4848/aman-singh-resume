01 / Technical analysis

Map the sequence before naming a pattern

An uptrend is often described by rising swing highs and lows; a downtrend by falling swing highs and lows. A range lacks sustained progress in either direction. The swing-identification rule matters: changing the lookback can change your labels. Record that rule before reviewing results.

  • Higher high / higher low: a sequence, not a guarantee of continuation.
  • Lower high / lower low: a sequence, not a reason by itself to short.
  • Break of structure and change of character are descriptive terms with non-universal definitions. Specify exactly which swing and which close you mean.
02 / Technical analysis

Support and resistance are areas

Support describes an area where prior declines met buying interest; resistance describes an area where advances met selling interest. Previous-session extremes, repeated turning points and round numbers can attract attention. None is an impenetrable wall.

  • Use a zone whose width reflects the instrument and timeframe.
  • Prior highs and lows are reference points, not certain pools of executable orders.
  • A broken resistance area may later behave as support, but a retest is not assured.
WORKED EXAMPLE / FICTIONAL

In a fictional range from 100 to 110, a print at 110.20 is not automatically a durable breakout. Price can return below 110 before or after the candle closes.

03 / Technical analysis

Breakout, retest and failure

A breakout means price moved beyond a chosen boundary. A retest means it revisited that area. To study either, define what counts as a break, whether a close matters and how long the observation remains valid. A false breakout returns inside the prior structure instead of sustaining progress.

  • Context: preceding trend, volatility, nearby levels and scheduled events.
  • Evidence: closing location and volume relative to comparable periods.
  • Invalidation: a pre-written condition showing the idea no longer fits.
  • Alternative: the market can trend, range or reverse; consider more than one scenario.
04 / Technical analysis

Liquidity concepts without storytelling

Liquidity is the ability to transact size with limited price impact. Equal highs, equal lows and crowded stop locations are sometimes discussed as liquidity references. A quick move through a level is observable; claims about a particular institution intentionally hunting stops usually are not provable from candles alone.

  • A market-depth display is a snapshot and can change or cancel rapidly.
  • Order flow adds information, but does not eliminate uncertainty.
  • A setup is only a research hypothesis until costs, failures and out-of-sample behavior are examined.
