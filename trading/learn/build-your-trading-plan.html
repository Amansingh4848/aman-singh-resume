Build a written learning & trading plan

Bring context, rules, execution and review together without confusing a plan with a profit promise.

01 / Risk & process

Separate a learning goal from a money goal

A learning goal is observable: explain a contract, calculate a payoff or identify an invalidation rule. A daily income target does not make the market offer a suitable opportunity. Begin with what you need to understand and how you will check that understanding.

  • Choose one instrument family and one observation timeframe for a study exercise.
  • List the missing knowledge before attempting a strategy: contract, liquidity, costs and settlement.
  • Keep essential spending and personal financial commitments outside a speculative experiment.
02 / Risk & process

Write a one-page decision framework

A repeatable framework records the market context, the condition being studied, the assumptions behind it and the evidence that would contradict it. Include a no-trade condition. A plan is useful because it exposes decisions to review, not because it makes outcomes certain.

  • Context: date, data source, event calendar, trend or range and nearby reference zones.
  • Hypothesis: precise condition, observation time and alternative explanation.
  • Risk: fictional entry, invalidation, quantity calculation, friction and adverse-gap scenario.
  • Execution: order assumptions, partial fills, rejects and what happens if the platform disconnects.
03 / Risk & process

Run a paper exercise from start to finish

Record the plan before revealing the next prices. Document whether the assumed entry could plausibly fill, including spread and available information at that time. If a rule changes during the exercise, preserve the original version and explain the change rather than rewriting history.

  • Collect both fitting and non-fitting examples, not only winners.
  • Separate process adherence from the financial result.
  • Use a fixed evaluation sample and do not turn a few paper wins into a claim of live readiness.
WORKED EXAMPLE / FICTIONAL

A fictional plan may say: observe whether a defined range is broken on a completed bar; do nothing during a scheduled release; record a failed break as well as a continuing move. This is a study protocol, not a recommended entry rule.

04 / Risk & process

End with a review, not a verdict on yourself

Review decisions, data quality, execution assumptions and uncertainty. A losing example can teach something useful; a profitable example can still rely on a bad assumption. Decide what to study next and what evidence would make you abandon the idea.

  • Use the local journal to record observation, evidence, alternative scenario and review.
  • Check your understanding with the lesson questions and work through losing payoff examples.
  • Read current official rules before any real transaction; seek qualified help for personal financial decisions.
  • The goal of this library is understanding. It does not certify competence or establish that trading is suitable for you.

PAUSE / APPLY / REFLECT

Make it your own.

Write your one-page paper-study plan. Include at least one no-trade condition, one adverse execution scenario and an explicit reason you might reject the idea after testing.
