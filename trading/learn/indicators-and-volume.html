Indicators, volume and confirmation

Moving averages, RSI, VWAP, MACD, ATR and the limits of derived signals.

01 / Technical analysis

Indicators transform data; they do not create certainty

A simple moving average is an average over a lookback. An exponential moving average weights recent observations more heavily. Both lag the price data used to calculate them. Short lookbacks react faster and may be noisier; long lookbacks are smoother but slower.

  • Trend filters need explicit settings, data source and timeframe.
  • A crossover can whipsaw in a range.
  • Combining several price-derived indicators does not necessarily create independent confirmation.
WORKED EXAMPLE / FICTIONAL

Closes of 98, 100, 102, 104 and 106 have a five-period simple average of 102. The average describes those five closes, not the next one.

02 / Technical analysis

Momentum and volatility answer different questions

RSI compares recent gains and losses through a bounded momentum calculation. High RSI is not automatically a sell signal; strong trends can remain strong. MACD compares exponential averages and a signal line. ATR summarizes true ranges, including gaps; it measures movement, not direction.

  • RSI: momentum context, not a universal reversal timer.
  • MACD: trend/momentum changes with lag and whipsaw risk.
  • ATR: compare the scale of movement; a high value does not say up or down.
  • Bollinger Bands: dispersion around an average, not guaranteed reversal barriers.
03 / Technical analysis

VWAP and volume need the right reference

VWAP is a volume-weighted average of traded prices over a chosen session or anchor. A price above VWAP is a relationship to that reference, not an automatic reason to buy. Volume counts traded activity; derivatives open interest counts outstanding contracts. The measures are not interchangeable.

  • Compare volume with similar times of day; opening activity naturally differs from midday.
  • A spot index is a calculation, not a single traded instrument with its own cash share volume. Know which volume series your chart uses.
  • Some currency feeds show tick activity rather than complete global traded volume.
04 / Technical analysis

Build a small, testable toolkit

Write down the job of each tool: context, volatility, participation or trigger. Avoid adding another indicator whenever a losing example appears. Change one research variable at a time and retain an untouched evaluation sample.

  • State the condition in plain language before coding it.
  • Include flat periods, gaps and event sessions in the review.
  • Check whether a signal repaints, uses future information or depends on an unfinished bar.

PAUSE / APPLY / REFLECT

Make it your own.

Calculate a five-period SMA manually, then compare it with a shorter average. Explain why an RSI reading of 75 cannot alone prove a reversal.
