Orders, spreads and execution risk

Why a correct idea can still produce a different fill or a larger loss.

01 / Foundations

Market and limit orders make different trade-offs

A market order prioritizes execution at available prices; its fill price is not guaranteed. A limit order restricts the worst acceptable price but may remain unfilled or fill only partly. Availability and exact handling depend on the venue, instrument and broker.

  • A buy limit cannot execute above its limit under normal order rules, but it might not execute at all.
  • Displayed quantity can disappear before your order reaches the venue.
  • A large market order may consume several price levels.
02 / Foundations

A stop is a trigger, not insurance

A stop order activates according to its trigger conditions. A stop-market can fill far from the trigger during a gap or thin liquidity. A stop-limit adds a price boundary but may fail to close the position. Order types and segment restrictions differ; confirm current broker and exchange rules.

  • A target is a planned exit level, often implemented using a limit order; it is not a guaranteed profit.
  • A stop placed in software may behave differently from an exchange-held order.
  • Know what happens after a disconnection, rejection, partial fill or exchange halt.
WORKED EXAMPLE / FICTIONAL

An example position bought at 100 has a stop trigger at 98. If the next available bid is 95 after a gap, a stop-market could execute near 95. The planned 2-unit risk was not a maximum-loss guarantee.

03 / Foundations

Friction belongs in every example

Net outcome differs from a chart’s entry-to-exit move. Brokerage, exchange levies, taxes, the bid–ask spread, slippage and funding or borrowing charges may apply. Rules vary by instrument and can change. Use official schedules and a qualified tax professional for personal tax treatment.

  • Estimate costs before evaluating a strategy, then stress-test worse costs.
  • Do not count an unfilled limit order as a successful trade in a backtest.
  • Track rejection and partial-fill handling in a paper execution checklist.
04 / Foundations

Make the platform part of the learning plan

Use a watchlist, a clear layout and alerts whose trigger you understand. Learn position versus order views, quantity units, validity, leverage settings and the broker’s square-off policies. Two similar-looking tickets can have very different consequences.

  • Check symbol, exchange, expiry, quantity, order type and price before confirming any real order.
  • Paper trading is for learning mechanics; it may not reproduce queue position, emotional stress or true liquidity.
  • Never share credentials or give an unverified person remote control of an account.
