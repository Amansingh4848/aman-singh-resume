Options: rights, obligations and expiry

01 / Options

Understand the contract before the chart

A call gives its buyer a right under the contract to buy the underlying or receive the appropriate cash settlement. A put provides the corresponding selling right. The seller takes the obligation. Exercise style, expiry, underlying, multiplier and settlement method are part of the contract, not details to ignore.

  • A call buyer can benefit from upward movement, but time and volatility also affect the premium.
  • A put buyer can benefit from downward movement, subject to the same additional factors.
  • Bought options can expire worthless. Sellers face obligations, margin requirements and potentially very large losses.
02 / Options

Intrinsic value is not profit

At expiry, call intrinsic value per unit is max(underlying − strike, 0). Put intrinsic value is max(strike − underlying, 0). A simple long option’s expiry profit subtracts the premium paid and costs. Before expiry, pricing depends on more than intrinsic value.

  • In the money: positive intrinsic value.
  • At the money: underlying near the strike; the exact classification convention may vary.
  • Out of the money: no intrinsic value, yet the option can still have a premium before expiry.
WORKED EXAMPLE / FICTIONAL

A hypothetical call with strike 1,000 bought for 30 has intrinsic value 20 when expiry settlement is 1,020. The expiry outcome is a 10-unit loss before costs, despite being in the money.

03 / Options

Time value, implied volatility and breakeven

The premium above intrinsic value is often called time or extrinsic value. Time remaining, expected variability, rates and other contract factors influence pricing. A rise in the underlying does not guarantee a call buyer’s profit if other factors move unfavorably.

  • Simple long-call expiry breakeven: strike + premium, before costs.
  • Simple long-put expiry breakeven: strike − premium, before costs.
  • Expiry breakeven is not a universal price at which a pre-expiry position becomes profitable.
  • Implied volatility is an input inferred from option prices through a model; it is not a direction forecast.
04 / Options

Indian index and stock contracts need separate checks

Do not assume that an index option and an individual-stock option settle the same way. Check the current NSE contract specifications and your broker’s margin and settlement policies. Lot size, expiry schedules and eligible contracts can change.

  • Know the quantity multiplier before converting points into rupees.
  • Delivery obligations, exercise treatment and broker cutoffs can change practical risk.
  • Uncovered calls have theoretically unlimited loss; uncovered puts can have substantial downside. A seller’s margin is not a loss cap.
