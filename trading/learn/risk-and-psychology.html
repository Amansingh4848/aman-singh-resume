01 / Risk & process

Translate an idea into a risk budget

A research plan specifies the instrument, entry assumption, invalidation, exit process and quantity. Position size connects a price move to a cash outcome. A small-looking percentage change can create a large loss when quantity or leverage is large. There is no universally safe risk percentage.

  • For a simple long cash-share example: planned risk per share = entry minus stop, plus an allowance for costs and slippage.
  • A model quantity is the whole-number risk budget divided by planned risk per share, subject to available cash.
  • Actual gaps, liquidity and execution can produce a larger loss than the planned amount.
WORKED EXAMPLE / FICTIONAL

Fictional capital 100,000 and a study risk budget of 1,000 with 2.50 units of planned risk per share produce 400 shares before cash constraints. This is arithmetic, not a recommended allocation.

02 / Risk & process

Reward-to-risk is not a probability

A 2:1 planned reward-to-risk ratio describes the distances to two assumed exits. It says nothing by itself about the frequency of reaching each. Expectancy combines the probability and size of wins and losses, less costs. An attractive average can conceal rare large losses.

  • Expectancy = win probability × average win − loss probability × average loss − average costs.
  • A 40% win rate with average win 200 and average loss 100 has gross expectancy 20 per trade; costs of 25 make it negative.
  • A 20% drawdown requires a 25% gain on the remaining capital to recover. Loss and recovery percentages use different bases.
03 / Risk & process

Correlations and leverage change the picture

Several positions can express the same risk factor. Three bank stocks and a banking-index position may be more concentrated than four independent bets. Futures margin is collateral, not the maximum loss. Options spreads can have execution and settlement complications beyond a neat payoff diagram.

  • Stress-test a gap, a spread widening and a platform outage together.
  • Avoid funding a speculative plan with money needed for essential expenses or borrowing you cannot afford.
  • Daily stop rules and position limits are process controls, not loss guarantees.
04 / Risk & process

Use a process that survives emotion

FOMO, revenge trading, anchoring and confirmation bias distort decisions. A trading journal should compare your written process with what happened, not merely celebrate profitable outcomes. A rule-breaking win is not evidence that the rule-breaking was sound.

  • Before: event calendar, relevant levels, written scenarios and no-trade conditions.
  • During: follow the plan, record changes and pause when judgment is impaired.
  • After: save both successes and failures, including avoided trades and execution mistakes.
  • Review a sample over time; one result does not identify skill.
