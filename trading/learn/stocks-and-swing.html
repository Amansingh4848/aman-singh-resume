01 / Stocks

A stock is a business claim

A share’s price reflects changing expectations about cash flows, growth, financing and risk. A good company is not automatically a good investment at every price. A price chart alone does not provide a full picture of debt, governance or dilution.

  • Read exchange filings and company results, not just summaries on social media.
  • Distinguish revenue, profit and operating cash flow.
  • Valuation ratios need comparable businesses and context; a low ratio can reflect genuine problems.
02 / Stocks

Context can move a group together

Sector demand, regulation, input costs and interest rates can affect related companies. Index weights mean large constituents can influence an index more than small constituents. Breadth asks how widely a move is shared.

  • Relative strength compares one series with another; it is not the same calculation as RSI.
  • A stock outperforming an index can still be falling in absolute terms.
  • Correlation changes through time, especially during stress.
03 / Stocks

Overnight holding creates event exposure

Swing and positional approaches face earnings surprises, corporate actions, announcements and global events while the domestic market is closed. The next opening price may jump past planned exits. A tight plotted stop does not eliminate gap risk.

  • Understand dividends, splits, rights issues and adjusted chart data.
  • Check scheduled company events before interpreting historical gaps.
  • Liquidity can deteriorate when everyone wants to exit together.
04 / Stocks

Build a research watchlist

Write the reason each symbol is included, the source date and the evidence that would contradict the idea. Separate business research from a technical timing experiment. Use paper observation first and review rejected ideas as well as attractive charts.

  • Record market context, company event, chart condition and invalidation.
  • Avoid survivorship bias: today’s successful companies are not the entire historical opportunity set.
  • Keep your research process separate from rumors or personalized recommendations.
