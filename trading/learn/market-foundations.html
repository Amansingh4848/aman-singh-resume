01 / Foundations

Start with the instrument, not the tip

A share represents an ownership interest in a company. A bond is a debt claim. An index is a calculation describing a basket of securities; it is not itself a share you can buy. Funds and derivatives can reference an index, but their structures and risks differ. A derivative obtains its value from an underlying asset or reference, with additional contract terms.

  • Stocks: business performance, valuation, liquidity and corporate-event risk.
  • Futures: a contract with obligations, margin and mark-to-market exposure.
  • Options: rights for buyers and obligations for sellers, with an expiry date.
  • Crypto and currencies: different market structures, custody arrangements and legal requirements.
02 / Foundations

Exchanges, brokers and the order book

An exchange brings together trading interests under its rules. A broker provides access; clearing and settlement determine how obligations are met. A screen price is usually a past transaction or a current quote, not a guaranteed execution price. The bid is a buying quote; the ask is a selling quote. Their gap is the spread.

  • NSE and BSE are Indian exchanges; their product and segment rules differ.
  • NIFTY 50, NIFTY Bank and SENSEX measure different baskets. A move in one does not mean every constituent moved.
  • Demat holdings, a cash trading account and a derivatives position are different records. Learn your broker’s workflow.
WORKED EXAMPLE / FICTIONAL

Suppose the best bid is 99.90 and ask is 100.10. An immediate buy may transact near the ask, not at the last traded price of 100.00. Larger orders can reach worse prices deeper in the book.

03 / Foundations

Trading styles are time horizons, not skill levels

Scalping involves very short holding periods and many execution decisions. Intraday positions are normally opened and closed within the session. Swing and positional approaches hold longer and face overnight gaps and corporate events. Investing typically focuses on a longer-term ownership thesis. More frequent trading means costs and mistakes can accumulate faster.

  • Choose a study horizon before comparing charts. A daily chart and a five-minute chart answer different questions.
  • Market hours, settlement cycles, permitted order types and contract sizes may change; verify them with the exchange and broker.
  • A course, a chart pattern or a profitable example does not establish that trading is suitable for someone.
04 / Foundations

Build an information hierarchy

Use exchange announcements and company filings for corporate facts; use official economic releases for macro data. Separate a verified fact, an interpretation and an untested opinion. Social posts and screenshots can omit losses, costs or timing.

  • Ask: what instrument, what timestamp, which source and which assumptions?
  • Do not send money, account credentials, OTPs or remote access to someone promising guaranteed returns.
  • Understand costs, taxation and complaint channels before opening or using a real account.
