01 / Foundations

Four prices, one interval

A standard candlestick describes the open, high, low and close over a chosen time interval. Its body joins open and close; its wick spans the extremes. If close is above open, the interval finished higher. That is a description of the past, not a forecast of the next candle.

  • A long body describes a larger net move relative to nearby candles.
  • A long wick shows an excursion away from the body; it does not identify who traded or prove manipulation.
  • A doji has open and close close together. Its importance depends on location and context.
WORKED EXAMPLE / FICTIONAL

A candle with open 100, high 110, low 96 and close 106 rose by 6 units over the interval. Its range was 14 units. Neither value tells you what tomorrow will do.

02 / Foundations

Single- and multi-candle shapes

Hammer, shooting star and pin-bar names describe body-and-wick geometry. Engulfing patterns compare adjacent real bodies. An inside bar has its high and low within the previous bar’s range. Morning and evening stars use a three-candle sequence. Definitions vary slightly between textbooks and platforms.

  • Shape: describe the actual prices and relative sizes.
  • Location: ask whether it appears after a trend, at a zone or in random congestion.
  • Follow-through: study subsequent action without pretending the outcome was knowable in advance.
  • Failure: keep examples where the expected direction did not occur.
03 / Foundations

Timeframes change the story

A small upward candle can exist inside a large downward daily candle. Higher-timeframe context and a lower-timeframe observation are not contradictions. Match the timeframe to the question. A screenshot with no axis, timestamp or surrounding bars gives limited evidence.

  • Start with a higher-timeframe map, then inspect a lower-timeframe sequence.
  • Compare candles from the same data source and session definition.
  • A still-forming candle can change its shape before it closes.
04 / Foundations

Standard versus transformed charts

Line charts simplify prices, often using closes. OHLC bars and standard candles retain interval extremes. Heikin Ashi and some other chart styles transform prices; their displayed values may not be executable market prices. Logarithmic scales compare proportional moves while linear scales compare absolute changes.

  • Use standard, time-based OHLC when checking simple order-fill assumptions.
  • Do not infer a guaranteed entry price from a smoothed chart.
  • Use the pattern atlas as a vocabulary guide, not a buy/sell checklist.
