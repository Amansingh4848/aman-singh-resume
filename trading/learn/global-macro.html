01 / Global markets

Follow a transmission channel, not a headline

A global event can affect Indian markets through earnings expectations, financing costs, currency movements, commodity prices or investors’ willingness to take risk. Different sectors may respond differently. The same headline can produce a different response in a different economic setting.

  • Higher oil can pressure some importers and benefit some producers; other variables can offset the effect.
  • Currency changes affect import costs and foreign revenues differently across companies.
  • Changes in global risk appetite can alter capital flows, without dictating every stock’s next move.
02 / Global markets

Expectations matter as much as the number

Markets react to information relative to what was already expected and positioned for. A strong data release may support earnings expectations yet raise concerns about tighter monetary policy. The net response is conditional, not a reliable one-line rule.

  • Compare release, prior value, revisions and a clearly sourced expectation.
  • Distinguish policy rates from market bond yields; long-term yields include expectations and risk compensation.
  • Do not infer causation from two charts moving together once.
03 / Global markets

Use an event map

Economic releases, central-bank decisions, earnings and geopolitical developments can change volatility. A calendar helps identify uncertainty; it does not predict the result. Time zones and daylight-saving changes matter when comparing overseas sessions with India.

  • India: RBI communications, inflation, activity indicators, fiscal policy and corporate results.
  • Global: major central-bank decisions, inflation and labor-market releases, oil developments and geopolitical risk.
  • Read official releases where possible and timestamp your observation.
04 / Global markets

Write two or more scenarios

A useful pre-session note separates known facts, uncertain outcomes and what would change the interpretation. Combine global context with domestic breadth, sector behavior, liquidity and actual price response. Never treat a macro opinion as permission to ignore risk.

  • Scenario A: conditions continue; what observable evidence would fit?
  • Scenario B: conditions disappoint or reverse; what would invalidate the first view?
  • No-trade condition: uncertainty or execution risk makes a paper observation more appropriate.
