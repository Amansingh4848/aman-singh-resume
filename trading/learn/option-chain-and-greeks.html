01 / Options

Start with the same underlying and expiry

An option chain groups calls and puts by strike for a selected expiry. Read the underlying reference, timestamp, bid and ask, quantities, last-traded price and settlement conventions. Stale last trades can make an apparently cheap option impossible to buy at that price.

  • Strike identifies the exercise reference; it is not the premium.
  • The bid–ask spread and depth affect executable prices.
  • Compare like-for-like expiries; different maturities contain different time and event exposure.
  • Our Nifty-style lab uses invented numbers, not a live feed or exchange trading signal.
02 / Options

Open interest needs both sides of a contract

Open interest counts outstanding contracts; volume counts traded activity during an interval. Every open contract has a long and a short side. An increase in open interest does not, by itself, prove bullish or bearish intent.

  • Opening and closing transactions can affect open interest differently.
  • High open interest is not a guaranteed support or resistance wall.
  • PCR, max pain and changes in open interest are contextual statistics, not reliable standalone forecasts.
  • Check the source’s units: contracts, lots and underlying quantity are not interchangeable.
03 / Options

Four useful sensitivities

Delta estimates sensitivity to a small underlying-price change. Gamma describes how delta changes. Theta estimates time-passage sensitivity; vega estimates sensitivity to implied volatility. These values come from models and move as conditions change.

  • Delta is not a fixed exposure or a guaranteed probability.
  • Near-expiry gamma can make exposure change quickly.
  • Bought options generally lose time value as time passes, other factors equal.
  • An implied-volatility fall can offset a favorable underlying move.
04 / Options

Volatility surfaces and events

Different strikes can show different implied volatilities, called skew or smile. Different expiries form a term structure. An event can raise option premiums beforehand and reduce them afterwards even when the underlying moves. Low premium does not necessarily mean low risk or good value.

  • Assess liquidity and missing or extreme IV readings before interpreting them.
  • Do not compare a distant out-of-the-money option only by its low rupee price.
  • Model estimates simplify a world with jumps, changing liquidity and execution costs.
