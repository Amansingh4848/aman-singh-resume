THE MARKET LAB / START WITH UNDERSTANDING

Behind every candle,
there’s a decision.

Trading is the buying and selling of financial instruments. Price changes as orders meet; charts summarize those transactions. Understanding the mechanics comes before taking any risk.

LAB
01—04
01 / CONTEXT

Trading isn’t investing.

Trading usually focuses on shorter-term price changes. Investing generally has a longer horizon and looks at the underlying business or asset. Neither approach removes the possibility of loss.

02 / OBSERVATION

Price action tells a story.

Read the open, high, low and close alongside swing highs, swing lows, zones and volume. A pattern is an observation to test in context—not a promise about the next candle.

03 / SURVIVAL

Risk comes first.

Leverage magnifies losses as well as gains. Learn position sizing, transaction costs, slippage and why a stop order cannot guarantee an exit price. Paper practice is not proof of real-world profitability.

THE READING ORDER

A small shape.
Four useful clues.

  1. 01
    The body

    The distance between open and close. A larger body means a larger net move over that interval, not necessarily a better trading opportunity.

  2. 02
    The wicks

    The extremes reached above and below the body. A wick alone does not reveal who traded, why they traded or where price will go.

  3. 03
    The surrounding chart

    Compare the candle with nearby price swings, the timeframe and volume. The same shape can occur in very different market conditions.

↳

A chart is a record of price, not a forecast. Start by describing what happened before speculating about what might happen.

A brief move above resistance can reverse into the range. A breakout alone is not confirmation of a lasting trend.

Strike & expiry: identify the contract.

The strike is the contract’s reference price. Expiry is when it ends. Compare calls and puts for the same expiry. At a 24,000 index level, the 24,000 strike is at the money; lower strikes have positive call intrinsic value, and higher strikes have positive put intrinsic value.

BEYOND DIRECTION / FOUR RISK SENSITIVITIES

An index can move your way.
Your option can still lose value.

Greeks estimate sensitivity with other factors held constant. They change with market conditions; they are not fixed promises.

Δ

Delta / direction

How much the premium is estimated to change for a small move in the underlying.

Γ

Gamma / acceleration

How delta changes as the underlying moves. Sensitivity can change quickly near expiry.

Θ

Theta / passing time

The effect of time passing. Bought options generally lose time value, all else equal.

ν

Vega / volatility

Sensitivity to implied volatility. Falling IV can reduce a bought option’s premium.

!

Know the downside before studying the upside.

An option buyer can lose the entire premium paid, plus costs. Uncovered option selling can involve much larger losses; an uncovered call’s loss is theoretically unlimited. Intrinsic value is not profit. These examples exclude lot size, transaction costs and settlement details.

Contract specifications, expiry rules and lot sizes can change. Check the exchange and your broker before making any real-world decision. This page does not recommend a strike, expiry or strategy.
